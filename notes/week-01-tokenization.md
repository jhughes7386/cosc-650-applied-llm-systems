# Week 1: Tokenization and Text Representation

## Readings

### 1. Tokenization: The Elegant Hack Powering Modern AI

[Tokenization: The Elegant Hack Powering Modern AI](https://craigtrim.com/articles/tokenization/)

An overview of why traditional definitions of words do not work well for machines and how modern AI systems use subword tokenization. Covers linguistic concepts including graphemes, morphemes, and lexemes, along with how BPE can capture parts of morphological structure.

### 2. The 30-Year Journey of BPE

[The 30-Year Journey of BPE](https://craigtrim.com/articles/bpe-history/)

Explains the history of Byte Pair Encoding, beginning with its use as a compression algorithm in 1994 and its later adaptation for neural machine translation. Also covers byte-level BPE and its use in GPT-2, including examples of how the merge algorithm works.

### 3. Breaking Text

[Breaking Text](https://craigtrim.com/articles/breaking-text/)

Examines how tokenizers handle real-world text and the practical decisions involved in segmenting sentences, compound words, special characters, code, and mixed-language input.

### 4. Why Non-English Speakers Pay More for AI

[Why Non-English Speakers Pay More for AI](https://craigtrim.com/articles/multilingual-tax/)

Provides a quantitative analysis of tokenization efficiency across 31 languages and explores how differences in language families, morphology, and writing systems can affect token usage. Highlights how tokenization can create unequal costs and access for speakers of different languages.

### 5. When Tokens Glitch and Users Attack

[When Tokens Glitch and Users Attack](https://craigtrim.com/articles/glitch-tokens/)

Explores potential security and reliability issues related to tokenization, including glitch tokens, token boundary vulnerabilities, invisible character injection, and adversarial tokenization.
